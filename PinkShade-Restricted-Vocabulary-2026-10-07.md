# PinkShade — Restricted Vocabulary (updated 2026-10-07)

Replaces the "Restricted vocabulary" block in *General knowledge → Language table* (last edited 2026-09-02, Section 2 of the General knowledge doc).
Built from: the live Language table, the Mood-Claims Ladder (2026-09-24), the נוהל 47 / Avulux trace (Paper Trail, 2026-09-10), and the product-classification note you pasted.

*Internal working list, not legal advice. Have Jonathan Atha and Gali Amir review anything you want to move from a restricted tier to an allowed one.*

## Why this update

1. **The classification line is the claim, not the product.** Blue-light/FL-41-style glasses with a generic claim are not devices in the US or EU. Avulux (480 nm filter, "diagnosed episodic migraine") became a Class I device under 21 CFR §882.5811 (product code QEO). Per נוהל 47, Israel follows the US/EU. So any wording that names a condition or a symptom puts the film on the Avulux side of the line.
2. **Gap found: the note says "relief," "photophobia," "migraine" and "light sensitivity disorder" were already on the list. They were not.** The old list had only "symptom relief" and "disorder". They are now added (Tier 1).
3. **"Contrast enhancement" was not on the list at all.** It is now a **conditional** term (Tier 3). It is closer to the line than "vibrancy/warmth", and Ladder Category J treats it as OK only when generic and measured.
4. **Added the Ladder's categories E–J**, which the old list only partly covered (named conditions, drug-class, authority claims, vision-performance claims, Hebrew traps, context escalators).

## How to read the tiers

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1. NEVER** | No approval or study unlocks it, or it names a condition (medical device in every market). |
| **2. BANNED until evidence/opinion** | Legal in principle for healthy adults, but blocked until a product RCT and/or a written regulatory opinion exists. |
| **3. CONDITIONAL** | Usable now only in the exact form described. |
| **4. Context escalators** | Not words. Things that make clean copy count as a restricted claim. |

## Tier 1 — NEVER (and named-condition / device-trigger language)

### 1A. Disease, treatment and condition words

- **Treat / treats / treating / treatment** · **cure / curative / cures** · **heal / heals / healing** (standalone) · **remedy** · **medicinal**
- **Therapy / therapeutic / therapy glasses / therapeutic lens / mental-health device** · **light therapy** · **color therapy / chromotherapy**
- **Diagnose / diagnostic** · **monitor a condition**
- **Prevent / prevents / prevention** (of any condition, relapse included) · **alleviate / mitigate / ease** (a condition)
- **Symptom / symptoms / symptom relief** · **disorder** · **condition** (as in "people with a condition") · **sufferers / suffering from** · **patient** (for a customer) · **indicated for**
- **Relief / relieves / relieving** (standalone). **NEW.** Use "helps" only inside an approved Tier 2/3 phrase.
- **Prescribe / prescription / prescription-grade** · **dose / dosage / "daily dose of colour" / prescription-strength** · **efficacy** · **clinical / clinical-grade** (as a descriptor)
- **Side effects / "no side effects"** **NEW.** Drug-comparison language.
- **Adjunct language:** "complements your therapy or medication", "alongside treatment". **NEW.** An adjunct claim is a therapeutic indication.

### 1B. Named conditions and symptoms (as a claim, a target audience, a keyword or a hashtag)

All **NEW** except where noted. These are the words that moved Avulux and Flow into device territory. No verb is needed. Targeting alone sets the intended use.

- **Photophobia · migraine / migraines · headache(s) · light sensitivity / sensitivity to light / light-sensitivity disorder / רגישות לאור · glare sensitivity** (as a condition)
- **Depression / depressive · anxiety / anxiety disorder · panic attacks · SAD / seasonal depression / seasonal affective · postpartum / "baby blues" · PTSD · OCD · bipolar · ADHD / attention disorders · insomnia / sleep disorders · burnout (as a thing to recover from) · mental illness**
- **Mental health** (as a product benefit) · **"for people living with…"** (any condition)
- **Hebrew:** מיגרנות · רגישות לאור · דיכאון · חרדה · הפרעת קשב · נדודי שינה · תסמינים · טיפול · מטפל ב… · מקל על…

> **Research framing is still allowed.** You can say "we're researching how people who experience stress or low mood relate to colour and light". You cannot say "helps people with…" (Ladder Section 7).

### 1C. Drug-class and treatment-equivalence (Ladder Category H)

- **Antidepressant** (and "natural / drug-free antidepressant") · **anti-anxiety / anxiolytic** · **mood stabilizer / stabilize / mood-stabilizing** · **mood elevator / mood-elevating / elevate mood**
- **Mood-altering / alters your mood**
- **Serotonin / dopamine boost** **NEW.** Mimics the SSRI mechanism with no evidence.
- **"Prozac for your eyes" / wearable SSRI** **NEW.**
- **"Alternative to medication" / "reduce or replace your meds"** **NEW.** A patient-safety hazard, banned even with data.
- **"Therapy you can wear" / "works like therapy"** **NEW.**
- **Hebrew traps:** **זריקה / זריקת אופטימיות** (injection), **מינון / מנה יומית** (dose), **נוגד דיכאון**, **טיפול שאפשר ללבוש**, **מרפא**.

### 1D. Pseudoscience-adjacent

- **Chromotherapy / color therapy** · **energy healing / healing energy** · **chakra / chakra-aligning** · **frequency healing / vibrational healing / vibrations** (as a healing mechanism) · **aura** · **holistic healing**
- **Hebrew:** **אנרגיות** (as in "אנרגיות טובות") leans toward energy-healing vocabulary. Use sparingly. **NEW.**

### 1E. Biomarker and physiological-mechanism claims **NEW**

- **Lowers cortisol / stress hormones** · **calms your nervous system / מערכת העצבים** · **regulates / balances your mood or emotions** (regulate = body-function claim) · **improves circulation / hormones / brain function**
- The 2026-08-29 spec version was already pulled back for the cortisol claim.

## Tier 2 — BANNED until a product RCT and/or a written regulatory opinion

Allowed only after the evidence in the Ladder (Sections 3, 4, 8) exists. For healthy adults and with no condition referenced. Today there is zero product-level evidence (claim-chain link 5).

### 2A. Mood language (the standing ban from 2026-09-01 stays)

- **Mood enhancer / mood-enhancing / mood glasses / mood lens** (and as a product name) · **improve mood** · **boost mood / mood-boosting / mood booster** · **uplift(ing) your mood / brightens your mood / lifts your mood** · **mood regulation**
- **Happiness / makes you happier / feel happier / happiness boost / brings you joy / feel more positive / feel-good lens** **NEW (the last four).**
- **Energizing / energy boost** **NEW.** Arousal claim.
- **Optimism boost** **NEW.** English is Tier 2; the Hebrew "זריקת אופטימיות" is Tier 1.
- Best wording once data exists: **"lifts your spirits"** (plain, not drug-like). Ladder Category E.

### 2B. Relaxation and stress (Ladder Category D — need an RCT of PinkShade itself)

- **Promotes relaxation / helps you relax / calming lens / soothing (as "soothes you") / calmer-feeling view** **NEW (calmer-feeling).** "Calmer-*looking* view" is Tier 3. "Calmer-*feeling*" is a wearer-effect claim.
- **Helps manage everyday stress / de-stress / stress relief / eases tension / unwind / wind down / restorative / recharge** (functional) · **supports focus / concentration / mental acuity**
- "Stress" is the only niche word that can ever become a wellness claim. Keep it Tier 2 until the RCT.
- Never place focus/concentration claims next to ADHD or attention disorders (Sparkles needed a device registration).
- US caveat: "mood" is not one of FDA's named wellness examples, so get a written opinion before using it in the US.

### 2C. Grey-zone emotional-health wording (Ladder Category F — needs a written classification opinion)

All **NEW** unless marked. In any depression- or anxiety-targeted campaign these become Tier 1B.

- **Emotional wellbeing / supports emotional wellbeing** (existing) · **self-care for your mind / for your nerves**
- **For low days / when you're feeling down / beat the winter blues / chase away the blues / "דכדוך"**
- **Calms your nerves / eases anxious feelings** · **emotional balance / איזון רגשי** · **builds resilience / helps you cope** · **burnout recovery**
- **"Light for your mind" / "mood light" / "אור לנפש"** (evokes light therapy)

## Tier 3 — CONDITIONAL (usable only in the stated form)

| Term | Allowed when | Never |
|---|---|---|
| **Contrast enhancement / enhances contrast / משפר ניגודיות** **NEW** | Generic, no condition, no "relief", and backed by a spectral report if quantified. This is the exact framing the US/EU non-device read and the נוהל 47 logic rest on. | Next to light sensitivity, photophobia, migraine, glare discomfort, eye strain, "for sensitive eyes", or any vision-improvement claim. |
| **Visual comfort** (was on the safe list) | **Under review.** On the safe list since 2026-09-02, but it overlaps the Ladder J "reduces eye strain / screen fatigue" claim and the "relief" trigger. Safer to drop it and use "softer, warmer-looking light" instead. | Any "comfort" claim that implies relieving discomfort. |
| **Perspective** | Replace with **view**. ("Tint your view" is approved.) | "Tint your perspective" (flagged 2026-08-30). |
| **Stress / relaxation** | As lifestyle flavour only ("a small daily ritual", "slow down and look around"). | As a functional outcome. That is Tier 2B. |
| **Wellness ritual / routine** | As a category label. | As a mechanism ("works through your wellness routine"). |
| **Filters / filtering** | As a spec fact in technical copy ("580–590 nm notch", "VLT 60–75%") backed by a lab report. | As a functional or protective claim ("filters harmful light"). |
| **Blocks / reduces glare, UV, blue light** | **Do not use now.** Moves into the sunglasses regime (21 CFR 886.5850; EU PPE 2016/425; EN ISO 12312-1) and needs test data. | "Protects your eyes", "UV protection", "reduces glare", "blocks blue light", "eye health". |
| **Reduces eye strain / screen fatigue** | **Do not use now.** The blue-light-glasses claim is not a device claim, but evidence is weak and class actions target it. | Any digital-fatigue or strain framing. |
| **Safe for driving / night driving** | **Do not use** until the film passes ISO 12312-1 signal recognition. The 580–590 nm notch sits on amber signals and may fail. | Any driving-safety claim. |
| **Sharper / clearer / improved vision, vision enhancement, corrects, corrective, shields your eyes** | Never for the film. **Corrective implication.** | |
| **Lens** | Say **film / overlay / sticker** for the product. | Describing the product as a "lens" or "contact lens". The statute lists עדשות מגע by name. The product is now an **adhesive optical film on existing glasses**. The old "snap-on" explanation is superseded. |
| **Device / medical device / equipment / ציוד רפואי** | Only if you deliberately intend the regulated path. | Product descriptions and outreach. |
| **Developed with optometrists** | Only if literally true. | Implies a vision benefit. |
| **Inspired by research on colour and light** | Accurate citations. Does not imply the product was tested. Usable now. | |
| **Research shows colour affects mood** | Background pages only. | On a product page. Next to the product it implies link 5, which has no evidence. |

## Tier 3B — Authority and proof claims (all false today)

- **Clinically proven / scientifically proven / clinically tested / clinically validated / proven** · **science-backed / evidence-based** **NEW** · **peer-reviewed** (of the product) · **guaranteed results**
- **Doctor-recommended / doctor-approved / psychologist-recommended / therapist-approved / "recommended by psychologists for their patients"** (the last two **NEW**) · **medically reviewed**
- **FDA-approved / FDA-cleared / FDA-registered** (registration is not approval, and implying otherwise is misbranding under 21 CFR 807.39) · **CE-certified / CE-marked** · **Ministry of Health approved / אמ"ר registered**
- **"X% felt calmer / happier"** with no documented study **NEW** · **"Studies on Zeiss / Futuremood lenses show…"** **NEW.** You cannot borrow another product's data.
- Unlock: each phrase needs exactly the proof it names, plus written consent for named professionals and disclosure of material connections (advisors Alon and Tal).

## Tier 4 — Context escalators (clean words can still be restricted claims)

Regulators judge intended use from the overall impression (21 CFR 801.4), so check these along with the copy:

- **Audience:** ads or waitlist screening aimed at people with depression/anxiety/light sensitivity.
- **Channel:** selling or handing out through therapists' or neurologists' clinics. "Ask your psychologist."
- **Imagery:** sad → happy before/after.
- **Names and hashtags:** "CalmLens", "Serotonin Shades", "משקפי מצב רוח", #depression, #migraine, #anxietyrelief. Also paid keywords such as "glasses for light sensitivity".
- **UGC and influencers:** reposting or even liking "this helped my migraines/depression". Paid or gifted creators speak for the company.
- **Adjacent citations:** linking a condition study next to the product.
- **Investor portal and pitch:** "targets the $X depression market" on a public page.
- **Disclaimers don't fix it.** "Not intended to treat any disease" next to a Tier 1–2 claim doesn't cancel the claim.

## Safe replacement vocabulary (Categories A–C, use now)

*adds warmth and vibrancy* · *richer, more saturated colours* · *a subtle warm / rose tint* · *softer, warmer-looking light* · *golden-hour colour, all day* · *see the world in a warmer light* · *tint your **view*** · *colours you'll actually notice* · *a small daily ritual* · *slow down and look around* · *a wellness ritual* (as a category label only) · *applies to the glasses you already own*.
Add numbers (VLT %, "boosts greens", peels off with no residue) only with a lab report. Don't claim "works with all lens types" until it is tested.
