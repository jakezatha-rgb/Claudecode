# PinkShade — Restricted Vocabulary (updated 2026-10-07)

Replaces the "Restricted vocabulary" block in *General knowledge → Language table*. Same six sections as before. Each now has **Allowed now**, **Restricted**, and **What it would take to unlock**.

*Internal working list, not legal advice. Cost and time figures come from the Mood-Claims Ladder (2026-09-24) where marked; the time estimates are rough planning numbers. Have Jonathan Atha and Gali Amir confirm before any word moves from restricted to allowed.*

**The rule behind everything:** in the US, EU and Israel (via נוהל 47, which copies US/EU classification), the **claim** decides whether PinkShade is a medical device, not the film itself. Generic wording keeps it an accessory (blue-light-glasses side). Condition or symptom wording moves it to the Avulux side (Class I device, 21 CFR §882.5811). Disclaimers don't cancel a claim, and audience, imagery, hashtags and channel count as claims too.

---

## 1. Medical / disease / treatment claims — RED, medical device in every market

Any of these turns the accessory into an implied medical device.

**Allowed now:** none. Nothing in this section is safe to use today.

**Restricted:**
- **treat / treats / treating / treatment · cure / curative · heal / healing (standalone) · remedy · medicinal**
- **therapy / therapeutic · diagnose / diagnostic · prevent / prevention**
- **symptom / symptoms / symptom relief · relief / relieves (standalone) · alleviate / mitigate · disorder · condition · sufferers · indicated for**
- **prescribe / prescription · dose / dosage · efficacy · clinical / clinical-grade · patient** (for a customer)
- **side effects / "no side effects" · complements your therapy or medication**
- **Named conditions and symptoms** (as a claim, audience, hashtag or keyword): **photophobia · migraine · headache · light sensitivity / light-sensitivity disorder · glare sensitivity · depression · anxiety disorder · panic attacks · SAD / seasonal depression · PTSD · OCD · bipolar · ADHD · insomnia · postpartum · burnout** (as something to recover from)
- Hebrew: **טיפול · מטפל ב… · מקל על… · תסמינים · הפרעה · מיגרנות · רגישות לאור · דיכאון · חרדה · הפרעת קשב · נדודי שינה**

**What it would take to unlock:** This is the medical-device route, not a copy approval. In the US that means FDA marketing authorization (De Novo, roughly $48K–$191K in fees, or PMA for depression, roughly $159K–$637K, plus the quality system, registration and adverse-event reporting). In the EU it means a CE mark under MDR with our own clinical investigation, an EU Authorised Representative and EUDAMED registration. In Israel it means אמ"ר registration through an Israeli registration holder. Each needs a pivotal clinical trial in a **diagnosed** population with ethics approval, an incorporated entity, a clinical PI, an adult sponsor (Jake is a minor), a regulatory consultant and product-liability insurance. Realistically that is multiple years and millions in trials, and the business changes to optometrist-fitted (the Sparkles model). Even after approval, only the exact cleared indication can be claimed. Not a near-term path.

> **Research framing is still allowed:** "we're researching how people who experience stress or low mood relate to colour and light." Not allowed: "helps people with…". Don't screen the waitlist for a diagnosis.

## 2. Mood / mental-state / emotional-health claims

Three sub-tiers. The 2026-09-01 ban stays in force until the evidence below exists.

### 2A. Allowed now (lifestyle and sensory, GREEN)

- **see the world in a warmer light · tint your *view* · a small daily ritual · slow down and look around · colours you'll actually notice · everyday scenes that look like a postcard · rose-tinted glasses, for real · your golden hour, on demand · celebrate the small things · designed in Israel**
- **wellness ritual / routine** as a category label only, never as a mechanism
- **Use *view*, not *perspective*.**
- Keep it about how things **look**, not how you **feel**. Don't pair a grey-day line with a sad-to-happy before/after image, because that becomes a mood claim.

### 2B. Relaxation and everyday stress — AMBER, usable only after a product RCT

**Restricted:** **promotes relaxation · helps you relax · calming lens · soothes you · calmer-feeling · helps manage everyday stress · de-stress / destressing · stress relief · eases tension · unwind / wind down · restorative / recharge · supports focus / concentration**

**What it would take:** No regulator approval is needed (FDA's General Wellness policy names relaxation and stress management as examples, and the EU and Israel follow the same line). The gate is **proof**: advertising law in all three markets requires a well-run randomized trial of PinkShade itself. Plan a pilot crossover RCT in healthy adults (about 40–60 people, PinkShade against a luminance-matched neutral tint, validated outcome scale, pre-registered, ≥2 weeks per arm), run with a university partner, ethics approval and an adult PI. Estimate: several months and a university quote. Add a lab optical report (ISO 12312-1) to back the FDA "low-risk" position, then counsel review of the exact wording. A single pilot supports an internal go/no-go; expect to need a larger or replicated study for unqualified wording. Don't put focus or concentration claims anywhere near ADHD.

### 2C. Mood enhancement for healthy adults — AMBER-RED, banned until study and counsel sign-off

**Restricted:** **mood enhancer / mood-enhancing · improve mood · boost mood / mood-boosting / mood booster · brightens your mood · lifts your mood · uplift(ing) your mood · regulate mood · happiness / makes you happier / feel happier / happiness boost · brings you joy · feel more positive · energizing · optimism boost · mood glasses / mood lens** (a product name is a claim)

**What it would take:** The same pilot RCT as 2B, but with a validated mood scale (e.g. PANAS) in healthy adults, **plus replication** for an unqualified claim. Then a written opinion from counsel. In the US, get an FDA Pre-Submission (free) or 513(g) request (roughly $4.3K small-business fee, 60-day answer) first, because "mood" is not on FDA's named wellness examples. Estimate: roughly 6–12+ months. When data exists, the safest wording is **"lifts your spirits"**. "Mood enhancer" reads pharmacological and stays last choice.

### 2D. Emotional-health, grey zone — RED for now

**Restricted:** **emotional wellbeing** (as a claimed outcome) · **self-care for your mind · supports your mental health · for low days · beat the winter blues / chase away the blues · calms your nerves · calms anxiety / anxiety relief / reduces anxiety · emotional balance · builds resilience / helps you cope · burnout recovery · calms your nervous system · lowers cortisol / stress hormones · light for your mind / mood light**

**What it would take:** First everything in 2C. Then a **written classification opinion before any use**: FDA Pre-Sub or 513(g) in the US, a borderline opinion from a national competent authority in the EU, and a written question to אמ"ר with the exact phrase in Israel. Estimate: 2–3 months for the opinions on top of the study. These phrases can never be used in a depression- or anxiety-targeted campaign, because the audience turns them into Section 1 claims. Physiological claims (cortisol, nervous system) additionally need biomarker evidence. Use "stress" rather than "anxiety".

## 3. Protective / corrective / vision-function claims

Pulls the product into a different bucket (FDA 21 CFR 886.5850 sunglasses rules, EU PPE Regulation 2016/425, EN ISO 12312-1).

**Allowed now:**
- **adds warmth and vibrancy · richer, more saturated colours · a subtle warm / rose tint · softer, warmer-looking light** (keep "looking")
- **makes greens and warm tones stand out** (must match the measured curve)
- **ultra-thin optical film · clear or grey base film · applies to the glasses you already own**
- **contrast enhancement / enhances contrast / משפר ניגודיות**, **conditional**: generic only, no condition, no "relief", no "for sensitive eyes", and no number without a lab report. The "no condition" limit is what keeps it on the accessory side of the line.
- **filters / 580–590 nm notch / visible light transmission X%** as a spec fact in technical copy, only with a lab report

**Restricted:** **protects / protection · shields your eyes · UV protection / UV-blocking / blocks UV · blue-light blocking · blocks light / filters light** (functional claim) **· reduces glare · reduces eye strain / screen fatigue · safe for driving / night driving · corrects / corrective · prescription-grade · improves / sharpens vision · vision enhancement · eye health**

**What it would take:**
- *Protective claims (UV, blue light, glare, eye health):* these make the film sunglasses-type eyewear. You'd need accredited-lab spectral testing to EN ISO 12312-1, and then product compliance with the US sunglasses regime and the EU PPE regulation (CE marking). That is weeks of lab work plus a compliance review, and it adds ongoing obligations.
- *Eye-strain claims:* not a device claim, but the evidence for blue-light-style strain claims is weak and class actions target it. Only use with real test data.
- *Safe for driving:* needs a pass on the ISO 12312-1 signal-recognition test. The 580–590 nm notch sits on amber traffic signals and may fail, which would also threaten the US "low-risk" wellness route, so run this test first.
- *Corrective / vision-improvement:* effectively closed. It would require the product to be a corrective optical device, a different product.

## 4. Pseudoscience-adjacent / alternative-medicine vocabulary — NEVER

Competitor research flagged this exact language (GloFX, VictoriousBeings) as a cautionary example.

**Allowed now:** none.

**Restricted:** **chromotherapy · color therapy · light therapy · energy healing / healing energy · chakra / chakra-aligning · frequency healing / vibrational healing / vibrations** (as a healing mechanism) **· aura · holistic healing · healing** (standalone) **· serotonin / dopamine boost**. Use Hebrew **אנרגיות** sparingly.

**What it would take:** Nothing realistic. These aren't regulated into permission. They are unsupported by rigorous trials and reputationally as risky as a false medical claim. "Light therapy" is a real clinical treatment, so using it implies Section 1. "Serotonin / dopamine" mimics the SSRI mechanism. We would need mechanism evidence about our own film, which doesn't exist.

## 5. Drug-class and treatment-equivalence — NEVER

New section split out from 1 and 2, because **no approval unlocks any of it**.

**Restricted:** **antidepressant** (including "natural / drug-free") **· anti-anxiety / anxiolytic · mood stabilizer / stabilize · mood elevator / mood-elevating · mood-altering / alters your mood · "Prozac for your eyes" / wearable SSRI · alternative to medication · reduce or replace your meds · therapy you can wear / works like therapy · prescription-strength · "your daily dose of colour"**. Hebrew: **נוגד דיכאון · זריקה / זריקת אופטימיות** (injection) **· מינון · טיפול שאפשר ללבוש**.

**What it would take:** There is no permission path. A device can be *indicated for* a condition but is never an "antidepressant", which is a drug class. "Replace your meds" is a patient-safety hazard even with data. Treat this as permanent.

## 6. Overclaiming credibility / false authority

None of these is true yet.

**Allowed now:** **inspired by research on colour and light** (accurate citations, doesn't imply the product was tested). **Research shows colour affects mood** is true as background science, but keep it off product pages, where it implies link 5 of the claim chain (zero product evidence).

**Restricted:** **clinically proven / clinically tested / scientifically proven · science-backed / evidence-based · peer-reviewed** (of the product) **· guaranteed results · doctor-recommended / doctor-approved · psychologist-recommended / therapist-approved · medically reviewed · FDA-approved / cleared / registered · CE-certified / CE-marked · Ministry of Health approved · "X% felt calmer" with no study · "Studies on Zeiss / Futuremood lenses show…"**

**What it would take:** Each phrase is unlocked by actually having the thing it names. "Clinically tested/proven" needs a completed trial (RCT plus replication for "proven"). "Science-backed" needs product-level evidence. "Psychologist- or doctor-recommended" needs a real, documented endorsement with written consent and disclosure of any material connection (our advisors Alon and Tal count). CE or Ministry approval needs the real conformity assessment or registration. "FDA-registered" must never be used to imply approval. Another company's data can't be borrowed unless the product is substantially the same. Roughly: unlocked as each study or approval lands, not before.

## 7. General regulatory-trigger words — use only if you deliberately want that regime

**Restricted:** **device / medical device · equipment / ציוד רפואי · diagnose / monitor a condition · contact lens · lens** (as a name for the product) **· sunglasses**

**What it would take:** These aren't unlocked by evidence. They are chosen by deciding to enter the regulated route (Section 1). Keep calling the product an **adhesive optical film / overlay / sticker on glasses you already own**, not a lens. The statute lists עדשות מגע by name, and the old "snap-on" explanation is superseded.

## 8. Context triggers (not words, but they make clean copy restricted)

Check these every time you publish, because regulators read the overall impression (21 CFR 801.4):

- **Audience and keywords:** ads or waitlist screening aimed at people with depression, anxiety or light sensitivity; hashtags like #depression, #migraine; paid keywords like "glasses for light sensitivity".
- **Channel:** therapists' or neurologists' clinics; "ask your psychologist".
- **Imagery:** sad-to-happy before/after.
- **Names:** "CalmLens", "Serotonin Shades", "משקפי מצב רוח".
- **Reposts and influencers:** reposting or liking "this helped my migraines/depression" counts as our claim; paid or gifted creators speak for us.
- **Investor portal and pitch:** don't call PinkShade "a depression product" on a public page.
- **Disclaimers:** "not intended to treat any disease" doesn't fix a restricted claim.

## Safe replacement vocabulary (use now)

*adds warmth and vibrancy · saturation · richer, more saturated colours · a subtle warm / rose tint · softer, warmer-looking light · golden-hour colour · see the world in a warmer light · tint your **view** · aesthetic / cosmetic accessory · a small daily ritual · wellness ritual (category label only) · enhances contrast (generic and measured only) · designed in Israel.*

Add numbers (VLT %, "boosts greens", "peels off with no residue") only with a lab report or a documented test. Don't claim "works with all lens types" until compatibility is tested.

**Changed from the old safe list:** **visual comfort** removed, because it overlaps the eye-strain and "relief" triggers in Section 3.
