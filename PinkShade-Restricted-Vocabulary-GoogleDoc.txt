PinkShade - Restricted Vocabulary (updated 2026-10-07)

Internal working list, not legal advice. Have Jonathan Atha and Gali Amir confirm before any word moves from restricted to allowed. The claim decides whether PinkShade is a medical device, not the film itself: generic wording keeps it an accessory; condition or symptom wording moves it toward the Avulux side.

What it would take to unlock: Only through medical-device authorization (FDA De Novo or PMA, CE mark under MDR, Israeli אמ"ר registration). Only the exact cleared indication could then be claimed. Research framing ("we are researching how people who experience stress or low mood relate to colour and light") is still allowed.

What it would take to unlock: No regulator approval needed, but advertising law needs a randomized trial of PinkShade itself: pilot crossover RCT in about 40-60 healthy adults, active neutral-tint control, validated scale, pre-registered, with a university partner, ethics approval and an adult PI. Plus a lab optical report (ISO 12312-1) and counsel review of the final wording. Roughly several months. Never place focus or concentration claims near ADHD.

Status: Mixed. See lists.

Restricted:

- protection

- UV protection

- blue-light blocking

- reduces glare

- night driving

- corrective
- prescription-grade

- sharpens vision
- vision enhancement
- eye health

What it would take to unlock: Protective claims (UV, blue light, glare, eye health): accredited-lab ISO 12312-1 testing plus compliance with the US sunglasses regime and EU PPE regulation. Weeks of lab work and a compliance review. Eye-strain claims: only with real test data (weak evidence, class-action exposure). Safe for driving: must pass the signal-recognition test first; the 580-590 nm notch sits on amber signals and may fail. Corrective claims: effectively closed.

Never use "FDA-registered" to imply approval. Another company's data can't be borrowed.

8. CONTEXT TRIGGERS (NOT WORDS, BUT THEY MAKE CLEAN COPY RESTRICTED)
Status: Check every time you publish.

Check:
- Audience and keywords: ads or waitlist screening aimed at people with depression, anxiety or light sensitivity; #depression, #migraine, #anxietyrelief; paid keywords like "glasses for light sensitivity"
- Channel: therapists' or neurologists' clinics; "ask your psychologist"
- Imagery: sad-to-happy before/after
- Names: CalmLens, Serotonin Shades, משקפי מצב רוח
- Reposts and influencers: reposting or liking "this helped my migraines/depression"; paid or gifted creators speak for us
- Investor portal and pitch: never call PinkShade a depression product on a public page
- Disclaimers ("not intended to treat any disease") do not fix a restricted claim

What it would take to unlock: Not a word list. Regulators read the overall impression (21 CFR 801.4), so audience, imagery and channel count as claims.
